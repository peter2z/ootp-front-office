OOTP Front Office 0.41.0 — everything since 0.40.1

Review — This release comes out of a sit-down review of the app against a real save: every page, eight GM scenarios, and the numbers behind each recommendation checked against the export they came from. The review found eighteen things the app said that were not quite true, ten places it was harder to use than it needed to be, and seven where it told you something without telling you what to do about it. The ones below are fixed; the review itself is in docs/review.

Fixed — The bullpen page never flagged a reliever on his third straight day. "Today" is the league's unplayed date, so the rule that looked for two appearances "yesterday and today" could not fire once a game had been simmed, and twelve relievers who had thrown on both of the last two days read green. Consecutive days are now counted back from the last game played: two in a row is red, one is amber once the outing was fifteen pitches or more, and the three-day pitch count covers the three days before tonight's game rather than two.

Fixed — The rotation assumed five men and "next start" contradicted the Schedule. A six-man rotation put its sixth starter under "spot starters", and three starters all read "next start: today". The rotation is now whatever OOTP projects, and each man's next start is read off the projected list against the club's remaining games, off days included. The Schedule, the Game Plan, the dashboard's "up next" and the Pitching page now take their probable starters from one piece of code, so they cannot disagree; before, the Schedule restarted its count at every series and could name the wrong man from the second series on, and the dashboard asked for the opponent's next starter rather than the one for our game.

Fixed — 40-man counts were wrong in both directions. Men on the 60-day IL were counted against the 40 (eight clubs showed 41 or 42 of 40), and active players who have used all three options carried no flag at all. The 60-day IL no longer counts and is labelled as such, and an active man out of options with under five years' service now says so: he is the one a call-up has to go through waivers to make room for.

Fixed — The dashboard chips now count exactly what their pages show. "0 roster issues" sat next to a page that listed six; "46 promotion signals" contained no promotions (27 watch, 17 blocked, 2 demote). The roster chip and the page read the same function, and the farm chip counts the signals that ask for a decision (promote, blocked, demote), with the breakdown in its tooltip. The extension chip counts the same rows the Contracts page marks.

Fixed — The Free Agents page was mostly the draft pool. OOTP marks the whole draft class as without a club, so 1,594 of the 2,766 "available now" were amateurs who cannot be signed, including the number 12 man on the Draft Board. They are left out with the same rule the board uses, and the page says how many. The "fills hole" badge went to 471 players, 470 of them below the 25th percentile; it now needs a man to clear that floor and to be at least as good as your best at the position. The salary column shows what a free agent was last paid, since OOTP exports no asking price, and says so.

Fixed — "Blocked" prospects were judged by scout grade alone. A 21-year-old hitting 1.072 at Double-A was blocked by a 37-year-old hitting .198 because the veteran graded 56 to his 41. The incumbent's season and age now enter the comparison the same way they do on the Contracts page, and the move says so: "would take Max Muncy's spot at 3B — grades 56 to his 41, but is hitting .198/.301/.385 this year and is 37". Every promote and watch signal now carries a reason; some watch rows had none.

Fixed — The call-up flow stopped halfway. The player card now says where a man stands on the roster rules ("Not on the 40-man · 35/40 · 3 options left"), a promote that needs a 40-man spot names who would come off, and the Prospects page links to the 40-man page when that is the case.

New — The lineup and bullpen cards can leave the app, and a red reliever names his cover. "Copy lineup" and "Copy bullpen plan" put the card on the clipboard as plain text, with tonight's game, the hand it was built against and the bench and unavailable men, for a message, a note or a forum. A reliever who is limited or out now says "use X instead": the same hand first, then the next man down the pen order, never a hurt arm and never another closer for the closer. The Game Plan is one click from the dashboard's "Up next" rows and from the Lineup banner, and the open plan is part of the Schedule's address, so a reload or Back lands on the same plan.

Fixed — The trade analyzer's "value swing" was a raw sum, so a star lost to two spare parts: Josh Jung against Ronny Mauricio plus a 0th-percentile throw-in came out in favour of the two-man side. Each side now shows its summed value, its best player and its surplus over replacement as three separate figures, and only the verdict is coloured. Replacement is the 25th percentile of the big-league pool for the player's group (position players, starters, relievers), which is also what stops a closer being measured against first basemen. A "quantity for quality" warning appears when the side with more surplus has nobody near the other side's best.

Fixed — Trade Fits ignored pitchers and offered your starting shortstop to fill your shortstop hole. A position is surplus only when the backup is good enough, and the starter is never the man offered; pitching now enters both needs and surplus, so a club can need or offer starters and relievers.

Fixed — The AI trade desk was told its stats were park- and league-adjusted when they were not. It now reads one line per level, each against that level's league and the park of the club he played for there, and the assistant gets a short briefing on the league's own rules (designated hitter, free-agency and arbitration years, options, roster sizes, rating scale) read from the save. When the assistant's lookups run out before an answer is written, it says so instead of returning nothing.

Fixed — Contract advice vetoed on tiny samples and sent mixed messages. A 24-year-old at the 97th talent percentile was told "hold off" on 125 poor plate appearances; another man got "Extend now" and "too little to judge" in the same cell; two more got nothing at all. The sample a poor season needs before it can overrule the value figure now scales with age and talent (a young star needs about 400 PA, a 33-year-old about 75), "Extend now" becomes "Extend (value only)" when the season has not weighed in, and every row with no action says why.

New — Contract advice now comes with terms, a deadline and an order. Suggested years and average annual value are the median of comparable deals in your save (same group, within 10 value-percentile points and three years of age, signed in the last three seasons; "no comparables" when there are fewer than three). The deadline is the in-game point the decision matters by. Rows are ordered by urgency: reaching free agency first, then arbitration, then extensions, each by value.

Fixed — Three payroll figures on one page did not reconcile. Payroll now, committed this year and next year's estimate were all different numbers with nothing to tie them together. A line under the cards now does: committed = payroll + dead money + the minimum-salary men in the minors, whom OOTP charges at the minor-league wage. Checked against three saves, 59 of 92 clubs come out to the dollar and 90 to a whole number of such players. Next year's figure is labelled as OOTP's estimate.

Fixed — The farm-system rank was a headcount: it correlated 0.97 with the number of players in the system, so the biggest farms ranked best. It now ranks the sum of each club's ten best prospects; adding fifty replacement-level players no longer moves a club.

Fixed — "Your tenure" lost its first season. OOTP leaves the manager's own record at 0-0 for the year he took over while filling in the finish and the title, so the page showed a title won in no games and a total that left the year out. The club's record is used for that year and marked as such.

Fixed — The two numbers shown for the same coach are now the same number. The promotion table compares a "seat score" (three parts teaching, one part handling), and the card shows it next to the raw ratings.

Fixed — Every server threshold assumed the 20-80 scale. Saves on 1-5, 1-10 or 1-20 got lineups that treated every glove as unplayable, draft advice that never fired, and a prompt that described a scale they do not use. Thresholds are now stated on the save's own scale.

Fixed — Some caches survived a re-import or a save switch: the trade desk's league median, scouting peer groups, batted-ball buckets and the farm pages' choice of grade column. They are all cleared on import now.

New — The header shows the league date next to the export's age: "League date Mon May 15, 2028 · data exported 55 min ago". After a sim the in-game date is the number to check, and the export's age could not tell you.

New — Pages have addresses. Every page, filter and player card is in the URL, so Back works, bookmarks work, a dashboard chip opens its page already filtered, and a player card can be sent to somebody.

New — Method paragraphs fold behind "How this works" so the table is the first thing on the page; the toggle remembers whether you opened it.

Fixed — The app can be worked from the keyboard and read by a screen reader. Every player link is named after its player instead of "Open player card"; the player card is a dialog that keeps focus and closes on Escape; glossary tooltips open on focus as well as hover; the menus take arrow keys; and the ✕, ← and ⚙ buttons say what they do. Column pickers announce themselves as dialogs.

New — Transactions can load more than 200 and jump to a date. Player Search and the Draft Board load a hundred rows at a time instead of everything, with "Show 100 more" underneath; both now draw in under half a second where each took more than five.

New — The assistant can act, a little: it can watch a player, drop one from the watchlist, and file a note, and it says what it did. Stop now stops the model as well as the browser request.

Fixed — When Ollama was not running, the chat said "Something went wrong". It now says it could not reach Ollama and to check that it is running and that the address in Settings is right, and docs/OLLAMA.md says which models can use the assistant's tools (llama3.1 and qwen2.5 can; dolphin3 and gemma2 cannot).

Fixed — Smaller things. Value and talent percentiles on the Contracts page and the player card were unreadable on the light theme; the widest tables scroll in their own box instead of dragging the page sideways; long names on the depth chart are cut with an ellipsis instead of running into the grades; filter drop-downs and the Plan buttons have names a screen reader can say, and a skip link goes straight to the page; the Lineup banner writes the date the way the header does; negative money reads -$16.5M, not $-16.5M; "43th" is "43rd"; "~1 days" is "~1 day"; flags no longer run together as EXPIRINGNO-TRADE; service time is written the way baseball writes it (11.027, with a tooltip); the injury report hides the "IL days" column when the export does not fill it; the draft board shows years to eligibility; Settings and the README describe the AI features that exist and no longer promise an import that happens by itself (the app notices a new export and offers to reload it); the folder placeholder on Windows is a Windows path; the Development and Watchlist pages explain that history belongs to the save; a league without free agency says so instead of listing expiring deals; the Windows-only test failure is fixed; the API port is OOTP_FO_PORT rather than the generic PORT, which tools kept setting to the UI's port; and npm start works from cmd.exe.
