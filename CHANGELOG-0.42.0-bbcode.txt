[*][b]New[/b] — [b]The Org Planner.[/b] One page for the whole farm: each level with its roster today, the roster the rules and your size targets say it should carry, whether it has its catchers, shortstop and centre-field cover, its starters and relievers, and its pitching staff as the ratings class it — the rotation in order and the bullpen with the closer first, and where stamina and pitch count leave a man between the two, OOTP's own starter and reliever values settle it. Below that, the moves to get there: promotions, demotions, call-ups, 40-man protections, trades and releases, each with the player, from and to, the reasons as sentences, the OOTP screen to do it on and the deadline. Service caps per level, the international complex age limit, options, the 40-man and the injured list are checked first, so nothing it recommends makes an invalid roster; the caps are OOTP's standard table and editable in Settings, since the game does not export them. Your size band's minimum is enforced and its maximum only warns: only men with no upside and no job are moved to shrink a level, and a club below is never drained to fill one above. Readiness blends three seasons of production, OOTP's grade against the level's median man, and age for the level; where two men fit equally, the one who covers what the big club is thinnest at goes first.
The 40-man count runs through the plan. A call-up or a protection takes a place, a trade or release made before the Rule 5 draft gives one back, and every card says where the count stands after the ones above it. Protections go in order of fit; a man who earns a place but finds none is held, with his Rule 5 date on the card. Accept or dismiss each move and the plan recomputes around your answer; the next import says whether the move happened and where the man turned up, and a protection not made by its deadline says so.
It is quick. The first plan for a 324-man organisation takes about half a second after an import and is served from memory after that, so the dashboard's first visit went from six seconds to under one. Part of that is three indexes the importer now builds; it builds whatever index is missing when the app starts, so a database imported by an older version gets them without a re-import.
